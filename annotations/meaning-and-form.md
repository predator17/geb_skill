# Unit: Chapter II — Meaning and Form in Mathematics
**PDF pages**: 90+
**Printed folio**: ~46

## Key terms
- **pq-system**: A simple formal system whose theorems can be interpreted as statements about addition.
- **axiom schema**: A mold that generates infinitely many axioms (x p - q x - where x is hyphens only).
- **isomorphism**: The correspondence that allows strings to be read as meaningful statements about numbers.
- **decision procedure**: A mechanical method that determines whether any given string is a theorem.

## Concept unpacking
- The pq-system has a clear decision procedure: the first two hyphen groups must add up in length to the third.
- Meaning is not intrinsic to the symbols; it arises when an isomorphism is discovered between the formal system and an external domain (arithmetic).
- The chapter explores how form and meaning can coincide or diverge, preparing the ground for later discussions of consistency and incompleteness.

## Author-stated intentions
- To present a new, even simpler formal system that illustrates many of the book’s central ideas about meaning, interpretation, and formal systems.
- To show that words and thoughts may or may not follow formal rules — the central problem of the book.

## Connections to thesis
- Establishes the basic mechanism by which formal systems acquire meaning through isomorphisms.
- Sets up the later contrast between consistent systems that capture truth and those that do not.
