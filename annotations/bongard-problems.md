## Key terms
- **Bongard problems (BPs)**: Visual pattern-recognition puzzles invented by Mikhail Bongard in which one must discover the rule that distinguishes two sets of figures.
- **concept network**: A hierarchy of concepts and links used to represent the knowledge needed to solve the problems.
- **slipping**: Moving from one description to another (often the crux of the solution).

## Concept unpacking
- Bongard problems serve as a miniature world in which “science” is done: finding regularities, inventing concepts, and shifting levels of description.
- Solving them requires both low-level feature detection and high-level conceptual insight; the same figure can be described in many ways.
- They illustrate the difficulty of building systems that can discover new concepts and slip between descriptions.

## Author-stated intentions
- To use Bongard problems as a concrete arena for discussing knowledge representation, pattern recognition, and the discovery of conceptual skeletons.
- To show how the same ideas reappear in the discussion of creativity and analogy.

## Connections to thesis
- Links levels of description, active symbols, and the flexibility required for intelligent pattern recognition.
- Continues the exploration of how formal or mechanical systems can approach human-like insight.
