# Unit: The Turing Test (Chapter XVIII — Artificial Intelligence: Retrospects)
**PDF pages**: 638+
**Printed folio**: ~595

## Key terms
- **Turing Test (imitation game)**: A proposal by Alan Turing for deciding whether a machine can think, by whether an interrogator can distinguish it from a human in a text-only conversation.
- **ELIZA-effect**: The tendency of people to attribute intelligence to simplistic programs that give the appearance of understanding.

## Concept unpacking
- Turing replaces the vague question “Can machines think?” with the operational imitation game.
- Hofstadter discusses both the original proposal and later criticisms (including Weizenbaum’s observations about ELIZA).
- The test is treated as a reasonable criterion for the presence of thought, while acknowledging its limitations and the role of the interrogator.

## Author-stated intentions
- To open the AI retrospects chapter with Turing’s famous criterion and examine its implications and revisions.
- To connect the test to broader questions of mind, consciousness, and the Church-Turing Thesis.

## Connections to thesis
- Links formal systems, computability, and the possibility of mechanical thought.
- Prepares the ground for later discussions of AI prospects and consciousness.
