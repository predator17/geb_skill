# Unit: Dialogue — The Magnificrab, Indeed
**PDF pages**: ~595–601
**Printed folios**: ~552–558

## Key terms
- **Magnificrab**: Pun on Bach’s Magnificat. The Dialogue in which the Crab treats formal number-theoretic statements as musical scores and judges them solely by aesthetic criteria, thereby revealing an apparent ability to distinguish true from false statements of TNT.
- **Piano Postulates / musical TNT**: The Crab reads sheets containing TNT formulas (e.g., Peano axioms or conjectures) as musical notation and plays them on his flute. Achilles notices the isomorphism; the Crab insists he is only playing music.
- **beauty vs. truth**: The Crab claims his judgments are purely aesthetic. Achilles observes that the pieces the Crab finds beautiful correspond to true statements and the ones he rejects correspond to false ones, raising the question of whether the Crab has an infallible decision procedure for number theory.

## Concept unpacking
The characters walk and converse about a supposed Oriental mathematician (Najunamar / Ramanujan) whose results the Crab verified only after a probability argument convinced him they must be false. The Crab then plays short TNT formulas as flute music. Achilles experiments by writing his own formulas; the Crab’s aesthetic reactions systematically track the mathematical truth-value of the formulas (including a Pythagorean statement about the impossibility of a square root of 2 being rational). The Dialogue therefore stages the tension between an informal, aesthetic “intuition” and the formal limits proved by Church’s Theorem and the Tarski–Church–Turing Theorem. It prepares the subsequent chapter’s discussion of whether any mental process that sorts numbers can be captured by a FlooP program.

## Author-stated intentions
To present a vivid, multi-level illustration of the possibility that aesthetic judgment might coincide with mathematical truth, and thereby to dramatize the questions surrounding Church’s Thesis and the limits of formal systems.
