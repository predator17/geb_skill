# Unit: Chapter XVII — Church, Turing, Tarski, and Others
**PDF pages**: 602+
**Printed folio**: ~559

## Key terms
- **formal vs. informal systems**: The low-level, rule-governed substrate versus the high-level, flexible behavior that emerges from it.
- **Church’s Theorem**: There is no infallible mechanical method for distinguishing theorems of TNT from non-theorems.
- **Turing machines**: Abstract models of computation that capture the power of any mechanical procedure.
- **Tarski’s Theorem**: Truth for a sufficiently powerful formal system cannot be defined within that system.

## Concept unpacking
- Every aspect of thinking can be viewed as a high-level description of a system that is governed at a lower level by simple formal rules.
- Understanding a complex system such as a brain requires successive levels of chunking, each of which loses some precision.
- The chapter connects incompleteness, undecidability, and the undefinability of truth.

## Author-stated intentions
- To develop one of the main theses of the book: that thinking can be viewed as a high-level description of an underlying formal system.
- To present the results of Church, Turing, Tarski, and others that delimit what formal systems and mechanical procedures can achieve.

## Connections to thesis
- Ties together Gödel incompleteness, computability theory, and the question of whether minds can be fully formalized.
- Prepares the ground for the discussions of artificial intelligence that follow.
