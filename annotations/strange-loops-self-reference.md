# Unit: Strange Loops — Gödel's Theorem as Metaphor for Mind
**PDF pages**: 739–740
**Printed folio**: ~696–697

## Key terms
- **self-image**: Formal systems (and people) that can represent aspects of themselves.
- **limitative theorems**: Gödel, Church, Turing, Tarski — all suggest incompleteness of self-representation.
- **introspection**: The problem of judging one's own sanity or understanding one's own mind.

## Concept unpacking
- Gödel's Theorem shows fundamental limitations for consistent formal systems that have self-images.
- Metaphorical application to psychology: one cannot fully understand one's own mind/brain once self-representation reaches a critical point.
- Either one is incomplete by hypothesis (low fidelity) or the limitative theorems apply (high fidelity).

## Author-stated intentions
- To explore whether Gödel's Theorem has analogues in psychology and self-knowledge.
- To caution that literal translation is unjustifiable; metaphorical use can be suggestive.

## Connections to thesis
- Strange loops in consciousness and the limits of self-representation.
