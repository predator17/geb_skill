# Unit: Chapter VI — The Location of Meaning
**PDF pages**: 202+
**Printed folio**: ~158

## Key terms
- **information-bearer**: A structure that carries information (e.g., a record, a DNA molecule, a theorem).
- **information-revealer**: A mechanism or interpretation that extracts the information (e.g., a record player, epigenesis, an isomorphism).
- **exotic vs. prosaic isomorphism**: An exotic isomorphism is highly nontrivial to divide into corresponding parts; a prosaic one maps parts easily.
- **genotype / phenotype**: The genetic information versus the resulting organism; the mapping between them is exotic.

## Concept unpacking
- Meaning is not always located solely inside a message; it can arise from the interaction of the message with a decoder or mind.
- Some messages appear to have more universal or objective meaning when the required decoding machinery is simple or widely shared.
- DNA provides a key biological example of an exotic isomorphism between genotype and phenotype.

## Author-stated intentions
- To discuss how meaning is split among coded message, decoder, and receiver.
- To explore whether meaning can be inherent in a message or is always manufactured by interaction with a mind or mechanism.

## Connections to thesis
- Deepens the earlier discussion of isomorphisms and meaning.
- Links formal systems, music, language, and molecular biology through the question of where meaning resides.
