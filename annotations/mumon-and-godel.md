# Unit: Chapter IX — Mumon and Gödel
**PDF pages**: 290+
**Printed folio**: ~246

## Key terms
- **Zen**: Intellectual quicksand; no way to characterize it fully with words.
- **kōan**: Trigger for enlightenment; verbal but meant to break ordinary logic.
- **Mumonkan**: The Gateless Gate, compiled by Mumon.
- **MU**: The famous kōan answer that unasks the question.

## Concept unpacking
- Zen resists enclosure by words; kōans and Mumon's commentaries are intentionally opaque or paradoxical.
- The chapter uses Zen as a bridge to Gödelian self-reference and the limits of formal systems.
- Mumon's comments can be read on multiple levels, sometimes self-referential.

## Author-stated intentions
- To convey the cluster of reactions to Zen (tantalizing, infuriating, humorous) and show how it leads directly to Gödelian matters.
- To illustrate that words and truth are incompatible in certain domains, paralleling incompleteness.

## Structural notes
- The chapter opens with the author's personal struggle with Zen since hearing Jōshū's MU.
