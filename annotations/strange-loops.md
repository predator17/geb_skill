# Unit: Chapter XX — Strange Loops, Or Tangled Hierarchies
**PDF pages**: 727+
**Printed folio**: ~684

## Key terms
- **strange loop**: A hierarchy in which moving through levels returns you to the starting level (tangled hierarchy).
- **tangled hierarchy**: A system containing a strange loop.
- **originality / will**: Samuel's argument that machines cannot possess originality because of infinite regress or closed loops.

## Concept unpacking
- The chapter opens by examining whether machines can possess originality, using Samuel's 1960 rebuttal to Wiener.
- Samuel's position is analogous to the Tortoise in the Two-Part Invention: any mechanical instantiation of will or reasoning seems to require an infinite regress.
- Hofstadter argues the fallacy is the assumption that a machine needs a rule to apply its rules; the lowest-level rules are embedded in hardware.

## Author-stated intentions
- To wind up the book's themes of hierarchical systems, self-reference, and the possibility of mechanical mind.
- To show that the Carroll Dialogue does not establish a fundamental difference between people and machines.

## Connections to thesis
- Strange loops are the central metaphor of the book for how selfhood and consciousness arise.
- The chapter brings together formal systems, music, art, and AI.
