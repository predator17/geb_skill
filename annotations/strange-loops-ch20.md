# Unit: Chapter XX — Strange Loops, Or Tangled Hierarchies
**PDF pages**: 727+
**Printed folio**: ~684

## Key terms
- **strange loop / tangled hierarchy**: A hierarchical system in which moving up or down the levels eventually returns one to the starting level.
- **invulnerable systems**: Systems that cannot be forced into self-reference or that remain consistent despite it.
- **originality in machines**: The question of whether a machine can possess genuine originality.

## Concept unpacking
- The chapter winds up the book’s central themes: hierarchical systems, self-reference, and the possibility of strange loops in formal systems, art, music, biology, and mind.
- It examines whether machines can be original and how tangled hierarchies appear in consciousness and self-reference.
- Strange loops are contrasted with simple feedback and with systems that remain invulnerable to self-reference.

## Author-stated intentions
- To provide a grand synthesis of the ideas about hierarchical systems and self-reference developed throughout the book.
- To explore the implications for mind, consciousness, and artificial intelligence.

## Connections to thesis
- Culminates the book’s central thesis that strange loops and tangled hierarchies are fundamental to consciousness, self-reference, and the unity of Gödel, Escher, and Bach.
- Links formal systems, biology, art, and the mind.
