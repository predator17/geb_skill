# Unit: Typogenetics (Chapter XVI — Self-Ref and Self-Rep)
**PDF pages**: 547+
**Printed folio**: ~504

## Key terms
- **Typogenetics**: Typographical Genetics — a solitaire game that models molecular genetics using formal-system-like rules.
- **strands**: Sequences of the four bases A, C, G, T (playing the role of DNA).
- **enzymes**: Packets of operations that move along strands and modify them.
- **Central Dogma of Typogenetics**: DNA ⇒ RNA ⇒ proteins (simplified model of Crick’s Central Dogma).

## Concept unpacking
- Typogenetics is designed to give an intuition for the processes centered on the Central Dogma while avoiding lower-level chemistry and higher-level classical genetics.
- It resembles formal systems (like the MIU-system) but introduces self-replication and tangled hierarchies.
- The game illustrates how information is created by the shunting of symbols in strands.

## Author-stated intentions
- To approach the molecular logic of the living state from a novel, typographical angle.
- To provide a skeletal model that reveals unifying principles otherwise obscured by complexity.

## Connections to thesis
- Links formal systems, self-reference, and biological self-replication.
- Prepares the ground for the Central Dogmap and strange loops in genetics.
