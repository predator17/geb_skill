# Unit: Dialogue — Chromatic Fantasy, And Feud
**PDF pages**: 221–224
**Printed folios**: ~177–180

## Key terms
- **contradiction**: Achilles tries to force the Tortoise to admit that saying both “my shell is green” and “my shell is not green” is a contradiction. The Tortoise refuses to combine the two claims with “and.”
- **joining**: The informal act of conjoining two sentences with “and.” The Tortoise rejects it by producing the nonsensical compound “Politicians lie in cast-iron sinks.”
- **propositional calculus**: The formal system that immediately follows the Dialogue and supplies a symbol (∧) that behaves exactly as Achilles wished the word “and” would behave.

## Concept unpacking
The Tortoise first asserts that his shell is green, then asserts that it is not green, and finally refuses to accept any rule that would let Achilles combine the two assertions into a single contradictory sentence. The Dialogue is a verbal re-enactment of Lewis Carroll’s paradox of infinite regress: every logical step must itself be justified by a further rule, which in turn requires justification. By withholding the ordinary meaning of “and,” the Tortoise demonstrates why a formal system must supply an explicit rule of joining rather than rely on the informal word.

## Author-stated intentions
To show, in narrative form, the difficulty of forcing someone to accept ordinary logical connectives, and thereby to motivate the introduction of the Propositional Calculus in the following chapter.
