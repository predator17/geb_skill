# Unit: Chapter III — Figure and Ground
**PDF pages**: 108+
**Printed folio**: ~64

## Key terms
- **figure and ground**: The distinction between a positively defined set (figure) and its complement (ground).
- **recursively enumerable set**: A set whose members can be generated by a mechanical process (theorems of a formal system).
- **recursive set**: A set for which both membership and non-membership can be decided mechanically.
- **tq-system**: A formal system that captures multiplication.

## Concept unpacking
- Theorems of a formal system form a figure (positively generated); non-theorems form a ground.
- Some grounds can themselves be turned into figures by another formal system; others cannot.
- The chapter links this to art (Escher), music, and the later contrast between truth and theoremhood.

## Author-stated intentions
- To compare the distinction between figure and ground in art to the distinction between theorems and non-theorems in formal systems.
- To introduce the ideas of recursively enumerable versus recursive sets.

## Connections to thesis
- Prepares the ground for Gödel’s incompleteness results: truth can be a ground that is not itself a figure within the system.
- Links formal systems, art, and music through the figure/ground distinction.
