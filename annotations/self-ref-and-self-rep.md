# Unit: Chapter XVI — Self-Ref and Self-Rep
**PDF pages**: 538+
**Printed folio**: ~495

## Key terms
- **self-reference (self-ref)**: A system or sentence that refers to itself, either implicitly or explicitly.
- **self-reproduction (self-rep)**: A system that produces a copy of itself.
- **Quine method**: A technique for constructing self-referential sentences by having a description of a phrase and a template for building it.
- **typogenetics**: The formal system introduced earlier that models molecular self-reproduction and self-reference.

## Concept unpacking
- Implicit self-reference relies on the processing power of the surrounding language or system (the “iceberg” of meaning).
- Explicit self-reference, such as the Quine construction, makes the mechanism more visible.
- Remarkable parallels exist between linguistic self-reference and molecular self-reproduction.

## Author-stated intentions
- To examine the mechanisms that create self-reference in various contexts and compare them to the mechanisms of self-reproduction.
- To illuminate the connections between Gödelian self-reference, language, and molecular biology.

## Connections to thesis
- Deepens the book’s central theme of strange loops by showing how self-reference and self-reproduction can arise from simple underlying rules.
- Links formal systems, natural language, and genetics.
