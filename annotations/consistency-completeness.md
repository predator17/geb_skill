# Unit: Chapter IV — Consistency, Completeness, and Geometry
**PDF pages**: 126+
**Printed folio**: ~82

## Key terms
- **consistency**: A formal system contains no contradictions (no theorem and its negation are both theorems).
- **completeness**: Every true statement (in the intended interpretation) is a theorem.
- **implicit vs. explicit meaning**: Meaning that requires equipment to extract versus meaning that seems immediately present.
- **non-Euclidean geometry**: An example of how consistency can be relative to an interpretation.

## Concept unpacking
- The chapter explicates the Contracrostipunctus Dialogue and its levels of meaning.
- It explores the relationship between consistency and completeness, using geometry as a concrete example.
- Implicit meanings (such as acrostics or hidden isomorphisms) require additional interpretive machinery.

## Author-stated intentions
- To explicate the preceding Dialogue to the extent possible at this stage.
- To introduce the notions of consistency and completeness that will become central to Gödel’s results.

## Connections to thesis
- Sets up the tension between consistency and completeness that Gödel exploits.
- Links formal systems, geometry, and the multiple levels of meaning in the Dialogues.
