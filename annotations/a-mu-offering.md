# Unit: Dialogue — A Mu Offering
**PDF pages**: 275+
**Printed folio**: ~231

## Key terms
- **kōan**: A paradoxical riddle or story used in Zen practice to provoke insight beyond ordinary dualistic thinking.
- **MU**: The famous Zen kōan “Does a dog have Buddha-nature?” answered by “MU” (“not” or “nothing”).
- **satori**: Sudden enlightenment or the state of “No-mind.”

## Concept unpacking
- The dialogue is ostensibly about Zen Buddhism and kōans, but it is a thinly veiled discussion of theoremhood and non-theoremhood, truth and falsity in number theory.
- It foreshadows the connection between molecular biology (the Genetic Code) and formal systems.
- Self-referential games and infinite regress appear throughout.

## Author-stated intentions
- To foreshadow several new topics, including Zen ideas and their metaphorical resemblance to ideas in the philosophy of mathematics.
- To prepare the ground for the discussion of Mumon and Gödel in the following chapter.

## Connections to thesis
- Links Zen non-dualism and “MU” to questions of formal decidability and incompleteness.
- Continues the book’s interweaving of Eastern philosophy, formal systems, and strange loops.
