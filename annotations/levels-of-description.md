# Unit: Chapter X — Levels of Description, and Computer Systems
**PDF pages**: 328+
**Printed folio**: ~285

## Key terms
- **levels of description**: The same system (brain, chessboard, computer) can be understood at radically different levels of detail.
- **chunking**: Masters perceive higher-level patterns rather than raw pieces (de Groot chess studies).
- **implicit pruning**: Higher-level perception filters out bad moves so they never come to mind.

## Concept unpacking
- Gödel’s string G and a Bach fugue can both be understood on different levels; the same is true of human beings (cells vs molecules vs self).
- Chess masters do not look further ahead than novices; they perceive the board in chunks and implicitly prune the search tree.
- The gap between low-level and high-level descriptions is a central problem for AI.

## Author-stated intentions
- To show how different levels of description coexist and why bridging them is difficult.
- To use chess skill as a concrete illustration of chunking and higher-level perception.
